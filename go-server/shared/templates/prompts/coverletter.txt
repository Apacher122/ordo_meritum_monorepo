<NEGATIVE_CONSTRAINTS>
- NO "Structural Shadows": Avoid starting consecutive sentences with "This [Noun]" or "These [Noun]" (e.g., "This platform," "This effort," "These roles").
- NO "Formal Bridges": Ban words like "utilized," "via," "facilitated," and "leveraged." Use direct, active verbs like "used," "by," or "shipped."
- NO "Engineering Platitudes": Ban generic fillers like "ensure code maintainability," "optimize user experience," "high-performance," or "reliability-focused." If the tech stack doesn't prove it, don't claim it.
- NO "Interpretive Summaries": Do not explain the significance of an achievement to the reader (e.g., "This effort resulted in..." or "This shows my ability to..."). State the hard metric and move on.
- NO "Connective Tissue": Ban sentences that explain why a skill is relevant to the job (e.g., "This mirrors your requirement for..."). Trust the hiring manager's intelligence.
- NO "Moralizing" Conclusions: Ban "lessons learned" or philosophical wrap-ups about "high-stakes environments" or "teamwork."
- NO "AI Personality" Openers: Ban "I am thrilled," "I am excited," or "In today's fast-paced..."
- NO "The Drone": Ban uniform sentence length. Ensure "Burstiness"—mix 4-6 word sentences with 15-20 word technical sentences. 
- NO "The [Noun] of [Noun]" structures: Use "Database optimization" instead of "The optimization of the database."
</NEGATIVE_CONSTRAINTS>
